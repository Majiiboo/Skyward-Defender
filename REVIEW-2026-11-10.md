# Skybound Defender: review, 10 November 2026

Rudy asked: what do I think of the game, the heroes, the difficulty, and what would make people
stay, discover and spend. This is the honest version, with the test numbers behind it.

## 1. The difficulty test (what the numbers say)

I let the test bot play real stages (3 runs each). "Typical kit" = what a player's coins buy by
that stage.

| Test | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Normal, stages 1 to 60, a decent player, typical kit | 39 wins out of 39. The base hull never lost a single point. The hero lost almost no health. |
| Normal, stages 1 to 30, **nobody touching the screen** | 15 wins out of 15. Hull never touched. |
| Normal, kit 8 stages behind | Stage 10: 2 of 3. Stage 20: 1 of 3. Stages 30 and 40: 3 of 3. |
| Elite, typical kit | Stage 10: 3 of 3. 20: 2 of 3. 30: 1 of 3. 40: 0 of 3. 50: 2 of 3. |

What that means in plain words:

- **Normal mode plays itself.** A phone lying on the table wins the first 30 stages. This is the
  biggest problem in the game today, bigger than any menu. If the player is not needed, nothing he
  buys or upgrades feels like it matters.
- **Upgrades are not needed in Normal.** The bot never came close to losing, so a level up changes
  a number but not the outcome. Upgrades only decide fights in Elite and in the trials.
- **Elite is uneven, not hard.** It swings from easy (10) to a wall (40) to easier again (50).
- **A player who falls behind gets stuck at 10 to 20 and then it gets easier.** The curve is
  upside down in that stretch.
- **The pacing of cards is right now.** A stage gives 6 to 9 picks over 1 to 4.5 minutes. Good.
- **Stage length is good**: 1 minute early, 4 to 5 minutes late.

One caution: the bot never gets tired or distracted. A real player on a phone does a little worse.
But "wins with nobody playing" does not need a phone to confirm.

## 2. What I would change in the fight (in this order)

1. **Give the finger a job.** Today lifting the finger auto-aims as well as dragging. Make manual
   play stronger than hands-off (your own idea, queue 32: an AUTO button with a weaker autopilot,
   manual = full power). This one change makes skill, and so upgrades, matter.
2. **Threats that must be dodged or chased.** Telegraphed beams and lanes, a bug that carries a
   chest and flees, a weak spot that opens for two seconds, pickups that vanish. Things you move for.
3. **A hero ability button.** Each hero gets one big move on a cooldown (Rook an orbital volley,
   Frost a freeze wave, Ember a fire breath). It is the single best way to make six heroes feel
   like six different games, and it is a natural thing to upgrade.
4. **Pressure on the base in Normal.** The hull should finish most stages scratched. Then shield
   and repair upgrades, turrets and the Aegis card finally have a purpose.
5. **A smooth Elite ramp** once the above is in, checked with the same bot.

## 3. The heroes: do they fit?

Yes. Collecting characters with elements is the right spine for this kind of game, and it is the
part of the game people will pay for. The way they were built is sound: one table of heroes, one
function that turns a hero into battle numbers, pictures placed by code. Nothing needs redoing.

What is missing is not the idea, it is the difference between them:

- In a fight every hero does the same thing: fly, shoot up. Their passives are small numbers.
  The ability button (above) and the ladder power-ups (built today) are the fix.
- Six heroes is thin for a collection. Twelve by launch (two per element, as you already decided)
  gives the Store and the events something to offer.
- The odd ones (the penguin captain, the dragon-kin) are an asset. Lean in: a line of story each,
  a short unlock scene, a signature pose.
- The frame-based 2.5D look is fine for this camera. It does not need 3D.

**Built today:** the Ascend ladder. Every hero climbs the same rarity ladder (Common, Uncommon,
Rare, Epic, Legendary, then Mythic I to V), so a Common hero is never a dead end, and a rarer hero
simply starts higher. Each named step unlocks something you feel: a stronger passive, a free card
refresh, starting the run with his own card, the evolved look.

## 4. Coming back, discovering, unlocking

Ranked by how much they give for the work:

1. **The next goal, always named on Home.** "2 more stages: Lance Pylon", "Rook: 3 shards to
   Uncommon". One line, one tap to go there.
2. **A real unlock moment.** Full screen, the art, what it does, a Try button. The Ascend moment
   built today is the template.
3. **Stage twists.** The trial modifiers already exist (fast bugs, no turrets, thick shells).
   Use them on normal stages from planet 2: each stage shows one twist and pays a bonus for it.
   The same stage is never the same twice.
4. **Daily and weekly modes that rotate.** Monday a boss hunt, Tuesday a no-turret run, a weekend
   Horde with a leaderboard later. Needs no new art.
5. **A collection book.** Every enemy, card, hero and turret you have met, with a small reward for
   each page completed. Cheap, and collectors love it.
6. **A season pass** (30 days, free row and paid row). It gives every session a second purpose.
7. **Story in small bites.** The commander already talks at each planet. Add two lines at each
   boss and a hero line when he ascends.

## 5. Spending, fairly

Your rule is right: money buys speed, never the ceiling. In the order I would switch them on:

1. Rewarded ads: revive once, double the stage reward, an extra card refresh. No cost to anyone.
2. The Starter Pack, offered once, right after the first boss.
3. The season pass.
4. A monthly card (crystals every day for 30 days).
5. Hero shard bundles and the stage packs.
6. Skins (looks only), later.

The Store is ready for all of these; only Google Play billing is missing.

## 6. Look and motion: what still stands apart

I looked at every page side by side. Still in the old look:

- **Turrets and Drone pages** (old tiles, long list, two-line buttons that clip). Next on my list,
  with the rarity names from the hero ladder.
- **Workshop** (old merge rows).
- **The battle top bar, pause menu, card pick and end screen** (the old painted kit and old fonts).
- **Modes, Quests, Settings, Store, Heroes, Home, Stage Map**: now one family.

## 7. The code

Sound structure; two debts:

- Two files do too much: the battle (5,300 lines) and the Heroes page (3,400). They work, but
  every change there is slower and riskier than it should be. I will split them piece by piece as
  I touch them, never in one big risky move.
- The old look is still inside the code as a switch nobody uses. Once you have seen the clean look
  on your phone and are happy, I delete the old half (about a quarter of the menu code).
