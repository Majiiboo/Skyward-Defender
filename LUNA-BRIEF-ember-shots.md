# Luna brief: what Ember's weapons FIRE (9 pictures of fire)

Hi Luna! This brief has everything you need; no earlier brief or message is required.

## What this is for
Your nine ice pictures for the penguin are in the game and they look great
(`ice_shots_in_game.png` in this folder shows three of them in a fight). Now the same nine for
Ember, the dragon-kin, in FIRE.

Ember has four weapons and each fires its own kind of shot:

| Weapon | What it fires |
|---|---|
| Pistol | small, quick EMBERS |
| Twin pistols | FIREBALLS |
| Rifle | a steady FIRE STREAM, like a flamethrower |
| Cannon | one GREAT FIREBALL that explodes |

Today these are simple orange shapes drawn by the game's code (`fire_shots_now.png` shows them).
Rudy wants your painting in their place.

The game moves, turns, stretches and scrolls your pictures; you only paint the pieces.

## Open these first (in this folder)
- `ice_shots_in_game.png` = your ice shots in a fight: this is how the pieces get used.
- `frostshot_stream.webp`, `frostshot_snowball.webp`, `frostshot_burst.webp` = three of your ice
  pictures. The fire ones must be laid out EXACTLY the same way (same size, same place in the
  picture, same black background), only in fire.
- `fire_shots_now.png` = Ember's four shots today as code shapes. Only to show what each piece
  is for; do not copy the look.
- `ember_reference.webp` and `ember_weapon_side.webp` = Ember and her rifle, for her colours:
  gold metal, orange-red fire, a white-yellow hot core.

## Rules for every picture
- 1024 x 1024, plain pure BLACK background, black right up to every edge. No dragon, no weapon,
  no text, no frame.
- These are LIGHT: the game adds them on top of the dark sky, so black becomes see-through and
  bright becomes bright. Paint glowing fire: white-yellow cores, orange bodies, deep red edges
  fading to black. No smoke that is grey or dark (it would not show), no dark outlines, nothing
  solid and dark.
- Everything FLIES UPWARD: the front of a shot points to the TOP of the picture.
- One thing per picture, dead centre, with clear black space around it (except picture 5).
- The same fire look across all nine, so they read as one family. Our own design; never
  another game's.

## The nine pictures

### 1. `embershot_pellet` : one ember
A small hot spark, like a tiny comet: a white-yellow head at the top, a short tail of orange
flame trailing down. About 400 tall and 130 wide.

### 2. `embershot_pellet_big` : the charged ember
Every few pistol shots is a stronger one. The same ember, but longer and fiercer, with two or
three licks of flame curling off its sides and a brighter head. About 600 tall and 220 wide.

### 3. `embershot_fireball` : one fireball
A round ball of rolling fire, about 420 across. Lumpy, churning, not a perfect circle. A
white-yellow heart, orange flame around it, a few sparks coming off behind it (below it).

### 4. `embershot_boulder` : the cannon's shot
A big ball of molten fire, about 620 across: a cracked glowing crust like lava with white-hot
light breaking through the cracks, flame licking round it. It must look HEAVY. The game spins
it, so no clear top or bottom; make it roughly round overall.

### 5. `embershot_stream` : the fire stream (THE IMPORTANT ONE)
A strip of rushing flame that the game repeats end to end and scrolls, to make one long
stream. Think of the jet from a flamethrower: rushing orange-yellow fire with bright streaks
and small sparks carried along in it.
- It runs straight up the MIDDLE of the picture, about 320 wide, soft on the left and right
  sides (the flame thins out to black).
- It must touch the TOP edge and the BOTTOM edge of the picture, and it must LOOP: the top
  edge has to join onto the bottom edge with no visible seam, because the game stacks copies
  of this picture one above the other. The easiest way: keep the flame the same width,
  brightness and texture all the way up, with no start and no end. Your `frostshot_stream`
  did this perfectly.
- No bright blob at either end. Even from top to bottom.

### 6. `embershot_stream_end` : where the stream ends
The stream only reaches a short way and then breaks up. Paint that end: the flame comes in
from the bottom of the picture about 320 wide, the same brightness and texture as picture 5,
and billows out toward the top into curling tongues of fire and scattering sparks, fading to
nothing. About 800 tall, 700 wide at its widest. (Like your `frostshot_stream_end`, in fire.)

### 7. `embershot_bits` : four small pieces on one picture
Divide the picture into four equal squares (2 across, 2 down), one piece in the middle of
each square, each about 300 across:
- top left: a small tongue of flame
- top right: a glowing cinder (a small hot coal)
- bottom left: a four-pointed spark (a glint of light)
- bottom right: a soft round puff of orange fire
The game throws these around the shots and the hits.

### 8. `embershot_hit` : a small hit
What shows for a split second when an ember, a fireball or the stream hits a bug: a small
burst of fire seen from straight above, a round flash with a few short sparks flying outward.
About 600 across. Round, the same in every direction.

### 9. `embershot_burst` : the great fireball's explosion
The cannon's fireball explodes when it hits. Seen from straight above: a big round blast,
about 900 across. A white-hot centre, a ring of flame and sparks flying outward in every
direction. Round, the same in every direction; the game grows it and fades it.
