# Luna brief: what Rook's weapons FIRE (9 pictures of ENERGY)

Hi Luna! This brief has everything you need; no earlier brief or message is required.

## What this is for
Your nine ice pictures for the penguin and your fire pictures for Ember are in the game and
they look great (`ice_shots_in_game.png` and `fire_shots_in_game.png` in this folder show them
in a fight). Now the same nine for Rook, the cadet in the charcoal and purple suit, in ENERGY.

Rook has four weapons and each fires its own kind of shot:

| Weapon | What it fires |
|---|---|
| Pistol | small, quick ENERGY BOLTS |
| Twin pistols | PAIRED BOLTS, one from each hand |
| Rifle | a steady ENERGY BEAM, thin and straight like a laser |
| Cannon | one heavy PLASMA ORB that explodes |

Today these are simple shapes drawn by the game's code (`shots_now.png` shows them). Rudy wants
your painting in their place.

The game moves, turns, stretches and scrolls your pictures; you only paint the pieces.

## Open these first (in this folder)
- `ice_shots_in_game.png`, `fire_shots_in_game.png` = your earlier shots in a fight: this is
  how the pieces get used.
- `frostshot_stream.webp`, `frostshot_snowball.webp`, `frostshot_burst.webp`,
  `frostshot_stream_end.webp` = four of your ice pictures. The new ones must be laid out EXACTLY
  the same way (same size, same place in the picture, same black background), only in ENERGY.
- `shots_now.png` = Rook's four shots today as code shapes. Only to show what each piece is
  for; do not copy the look.
- `hero_reference.webp` and `hero_weapon_side.webp` = Rook and the rifle, for the colours:
  bright cyan energy with a white core, a little purple at the edges.

## Rules for every picture
- 1024 x 1024, plain pure BLACK background, black right up to every edge. No hero, no weapon,
  no text, no frame.
- These are LIGHT: the game adds them on top of the dark sky, so black becomes see-through and
  bright becomes bright. Paint glowing ENERGY: white cores, bright cyan bodies, thin purple edges fading to black. Clean and sharp, like a science-fiction laser, not like fire. No dark outlines, no shadows, no grey
  smoke, nothing solid and dark (it would simply not show).
- Everything FLIES UPWARD: the front of a shot points to the TOP of the picture.
- One thing per picture, dead centre, with clear black space around it (except picture 5).
- The same look across all nine, so they read as one family. Our own design; never another
  game's.

## The nine pictures

### 1. `rookshot_pellet` : one energy bolt
A short bright dash of energy, like a tiny laser bolt: a white point at the top, a cyan body, a short fading tail below. About 400 tall and 130 wide.

### 2. `rookshot_pellet_big` : the charged shot
Every few pistol shots is a stronger one. The same shot, but longer and fiercer:
a longer bolt with a ring of light round its middle and a brighter point. About 600 tall and 220 wide.

### 3. `rookshot_ball` : one paired bolt
A fat, short capsule of cyan energy with a white core, wider than the pistol bolt, with a soft glow round it and two thin lines of light trailing behind (below) it. About 420 across.

### 4. `rookshot_boulder` : the cannon's shot
A big ball of plasma: a white-hot heart, swirling cyan energy round it, small purple arcs crawling over its surface. About 620 across. It must look HEAVY. The game spins it, so no clear top or
bottom; make it roughly round overall.

### 5. `rookshot_stream` : the energy beam (THE IMPORTANT ONE)
A strip that the game repeats end to end and scrolls, to make one long stream.
Think of a steady laser beam with life in it: a bright white line up the middle, a cyan glow round it, faint ripples and small sparks of energy travelling along it.
- It runs straight up the MIDDLE of the picture, about 320 wide, soft on the left and right
  sides (it thins out to black).
- It must touch the TOP edge and the BOTTOM edge of the picture, and it must LOOP: the top
  edge has to join onto the bottom edge with no visible seam, because the game stacks copies
  of this picture one above the other. The easiest way: keep it the same width, brightness
  and texture all the way up, with no start and no end. Your `frostshot_stream` did this
  perfectly.
- No bright blob at either end. Even from top to bottom.

### 6. `rookshot_stream_end` : where the stream ends
The stream only reaches a short way and then breaks up. Paint that end: it comes in from the
bottom of the picture about 320 wide, the same brightness and texture as picture 5, and
frays out toward the top into thin fading lines of light and a few sparks, fading to nothing. About 800 tall, 700 wide at its widest. (Like your
`frostshot_stream_end`.)

### 7. `rookshot_bits` : four small pieces on one picture
Divide the picture into four equal squares (2 across, 2 down), one piece in the middle of
each square, each about 300 across:
- top left: a small ring of light
- top right: a short dash of energy
- bottom left: a four-pointed spark (a glint of light)
- bottom right: a soft round puff of cyan glow
The game throws these around the shots and the hits.

### 8. `rookshot_hit` : a small hit
What shows for a split second when a shot hits a bug, seen from straight above:
a small sharp flash of cyan-white light with a thin ring round it and a few short rays. About 600 across. Round, the same in every direction.

### 9. `rookshot_burst` : the cannon shot's explosion
The cannon's shot bursts when it hits. Seen from straight above: a big round blast, about 900
across. A white centre, a bright cyan shock ring, rays and small arcs of energy flying outward in every direction. Round, the same in every direction; the game grows it and fades it.
